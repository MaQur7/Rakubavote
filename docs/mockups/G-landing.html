How an election works

  1. 1

    Register

    Officials register each voter with a photo, collect any fee the organization has set, and print a voter card.

  2. 2

    Review and lock

    The register is posted for objections. When they are settled it is locked and signed, with a short fingerprint code that agents copy down.

  3. 3

    Vote

    Each voter checks in, receives a one-time voting code, and votes in secret on a tablet: one position per screen, with a review before confirming.

  4. 4

    Count

    When the polls close the laptop counts the votes, checks them against the check-ins and signs the results. Ties are recorded; the committee announces.

Built for every voter

  • Candidate photo, number and symbol on every row, for voters who cannot read
  • One position per screen, large buttons, and a review before confirming
  • English, Arabic (right to left) and local languages
  • Type a candidate's number when the list is long
  • "No choice for this position" is always the last option

Trust you can check

  • The register is posted publicly, then locked before election day
  • Who voted and how they voted are kept in separate files with no shared key
  • The ballot, the register, every batch of votes and the results are digitally signed
  • Every action by an official is written to a log that cannot be edited

Rakubavote does not print paper receipts, so there is no hand recount. Read what it does and does not protect against.

Who it is for

Schools

Head prefect, head boy, head girl and secretaries, chosen by every student.

Community associations

Chairperson, secretary general and finance secretary, with an optional registration fee.

Party bodies

Congresses and primaries with many candidates. Offered on the same terms to every party.

One small kit per station

The software is free. You need devices you may already have.

  • Laptop: setup, registration, check-in, results1
  • Android tablets: voting booths2 to 3
  • Wi-Fi hotspot from the laptop or a phone, without internet1
  • Power banks or a solar panel, for a full day3 to 4
  • Any office printer, for voter cards only1

Open for anyone to inspect

The full code is public under the AGPL-3.0 licence. Universities, tech groups and election observers are welcome to review it, test it and report problems.
